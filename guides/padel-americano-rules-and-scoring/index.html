Padel Americano: rules, scoring and how it works

By the developer of Smash! Padel Manager · Updated 17 October 2026

The short answer: a padel Americano is a social tournament where you change partner every round and every player is scored individually. Each match is played to a fixed number of points (commonly 16, 24 or 32 in total), you add your team's points to your own total, and the player with the most points at the end wins. Nobody is knocked out.

The rules in one minute

  1. You play as an individual. There are no fixed pairs. Every round you get a new partner and new opponents.
  2. Every match has a fixed number of points. The total is agreed beforehand, usually 16, 24 or 32. All the points are played, so a 24-point match might finish 14–10.
  3. You score what your team scores. If your pair wins 14–10, you and your partner each add 14 to your personal total, and the other pair add 10 each. Losing narrowly still earns you points.
  4. Nobody is eliminated. Everyone keeps playing until the last round, so the whole group stays involved.
  5. The winner is whoever has the most points when the last round is finished.

A worked example

You and Sam beat Alex and Jo 14–10 in round 1. In round 2 you partner Mia and lose 9–15.

  • Points: 14 + 9 = 23
  • Point difference: (14 − 10) + (9 − 15) = +4 − 6 = −2
  • Wins: 1 from 2 matches

Tie-breaks

If two players finish level on points, most organisers look at point difference next (points scored minus points conceded), then at matches won. Whatever you choose, tell everyone before the first serve so nobody argues at the end. Smash! ranks in exactly that order: points, then point difference, then wins.

How many players, courts and rounds?

The cleanest Americano has four players for every court, so nobody sits out. To make everyone partner everyone exactly once you need one round fewer than the number of players:

Which points total should you pick?

  • 16 points — quick rounds, good for big groups or short court bookings.
  • 24 points — a comfortable middle ground for most socials.
  • 32 points — longer, more serious matches when you have the time.

Pick a total and stick with it, because points from short and long matches are not comparable. Put a timer on each round so the courts stay in step.

What if the numbers don’t divide by four?

With 5, 6, 7 or 9 players, someone sits out every round. That works, as long as the sit-outs are shared fairly: a player who sits out scores nothing that round, so the final table is only fair if everyone has played the same number of matches. Smash! always gives the next game to whoever has played least, and its Fair Ending option projects each player’s missing matches from their average if you have to stop early or someone joined late.

Americano or Mexicano?

In an Americano the draw is a fixed rotation, so everybody partners and plays against everybody. In a Mexicano the draw after the first round follows the standings, so you meet players at your own level. Neither is better; they suit different evenings. Read how the Mexicano format works to choose.

Tips for a smooth night

  • Number the courts and tell people their court before the round starts.
  • Agree on the points total, the tie-break and the number of rounds up front.
  • Write scores down straight after each match, not at the end of the night.
  • Keep one person in charge of the clock and the draw. That is the job the app is for.
