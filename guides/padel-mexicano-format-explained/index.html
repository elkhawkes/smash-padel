Mexicano padel: how the format works (and how it differs from Americano)

By the developer of Smash! Padel Manager · Updated 17 October 2026

The short answer: a Mexicano is a padel tournament where, after a random first round, each new round is drawn from the current standings. Players are grouped four at a time from the top of the table, and in each group the highest and lowest ranked team up: 1st + 4th against 2nd + 3rd. You keep meeting players near your own level, so matches tend to be close.

How the draw works

  1. Round 1 is random. There are no standings yet, so the first round is drawn at random.
  2. After every round, rank everyone by total points. Break ties with point difference.
  3. Take the table four at a time. The top four play on Court 1, the next four on Court 2, and so on.
  4. Inside each group of four, pair 1st + 4th against 2nd + 3rd. This keeps the two sides as even as possible.

Example with 8 players

After round 1 the table reads, from the top: Anna, Ben, Carl, Dana, Eli, Fay, Gus, Hal.

CourtTeam 1Team 2
Court 1Anna + Dana (1st + 4th)Ben + Carl (2nd + 3rd)
Court 2Eli + Hal (5th + 8th)Fay + Gus (6th + 7th)

Play the round, update the table and draw again. The leaders end up facing each other on Court 1, and the players at the bottom play together too.

Scoring

Scoring is the same as in an Americano: every match is played to a fixed number of points (for example 24), and you add your team’s points to your own total. See padel Americano rules and scoring for the details and tie-breaks.

Mexicano vs Americano

AmericanoMexicano
Who plays whomA planned rotation, so everyone partners and faces everyoneDecided by the standings after each round
Match qualityMixed: you may get a strong or weak pairTends to be closer, similar levels meet
RepeatsAvoided by designPossible, because the table decides
Best forSocial evenings, mixed levelsCompetitive groups, levels that are close
Number of roundsNatural end when everyone has partnered everyoneNo natural end, so agree on it beforehand

Strengths and weaknesses

  • Good: competitive, close games, and it keeps strong players from steamrolling weaker ones for the whole night.
  • Watch out: the same partners or opponents can come round again, and a lucky or unlucky first round can echo through the early part of the table.
  • Fix: if repeats bother your group, play an Americano for the first rounds and finish with a Mexicano final, or simply choose Americano.

Variants

  • Team Mexicano: pairs stay together and teams are matched by the standings.
  • Singles Mexicano: the same idea, one against one.
  • Super Mexicano: in Smash!, the winners on Court 1 earn bonus points (2 by default, from round 3, both adjustable), which raises the stakes at the top of the table.

How many rounds?

There is no built-in finish, because the draw depends on results. Agree beforehand on a number of rounds or a fixed finish time, and make sure everyone has played the same number of matches when you stop.
